What this calculator solves

Use Adhesive Melt Rate Capacity to compare calculated steady hourly demand with a melt rate verified for the actual adhesive and equipment. It is a capacity screen, not equipment selection or control logic.

How to choose the inputs

Enter measured grams per pack, the planned sustained pack rate, a visible peak or process allowance, and an adhesive-specific melt rate from current supplier information or a qualified run observation.

InputHow to prepare it
Adhesive per packMeasure adhesive per pack in grams on a suitable scale and preserve the sample boundary.
Packing rateCount packing rate in the exact operating unit shown on the dispatch or inventory record; do not mix eaches, packs, cases, or layers.
Demand allowanceBase demand allowance on measured comparable work, state what the percentage covers, and review it when the process changes.
Verified melt rateEnter verified melt rate in kilograms per hour from a current product/equipment source or representative observation.

How the calculation works

Demand = grams per pack × packs per hour ÷ 1,000 × (1 + allowance %)
  1. Validate the Adhesive Melt Rate Capacity Checker manifest: confirm that adhesive per pack, packing rate, demand allowance describe the same Adhesive Melt Rate Capacity Checker pack, batch, or planning period.
  2. Calculate hourly demand: apply Demand = grams per pack × packs per hour ÷ 1,000 × (1 + allowance %) without rounding intermediate values for Adhesive Melt Rate Capacity Checker.
  3. Review the hourly demand breakdown: use the primary result for the stated decision and the secondary values to identify the input or constraint driving this Adhesive Melt Rate Capacity Checker result.

Worked example

At 2.4 g per pack and 600 packs per hour, a 10% allowance produces 1.584 kg/h demand. A verified 2.5 kg/h melt rate leaves 0.916 kg/h of calculated headroom.

How to interpret the result

Utilization above 100% is a clear review signal. Positive steady-state headroom still may not cover warm-up, cycling peaks, refill recovery, or restrictions elsewhere in the delivery system.

Common mistakes

  • Using tank volume as melt rate.
  • Using a water-like density or generic equipment headline without confirmation.
  • Averaging away short demand peaks.
  • Reading positive headroom as proof of process capability.

Assumptions and limitations

The checker excludes warm-up, viscosity, temperature profile, pump, hose, manifold, gun, nozzle, controls, maintenance condition, ambient exposure, and safe operating procedure.

Adhesive Melt Rate Capacity Checker estimate only: verify the Adhesive Melt Rate Capacity Checker hourly demand with the physical pack or operating record and the current requirements governing this decision.
