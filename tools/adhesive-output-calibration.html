What this calculator solves

Use Adhesive Output Calibration to convert one timed, weighed collection into observed grams per minute per active nozzle and compare it with a user-entered target. It is a measurement check, not a fault diagnosis.

How to choose the inputs

Collect under the equipment manufacturer’s approved procedure, record total grams, exact seconds, and active nozzle count, and use the target and tolerance from a controlled setup record. Repeat samples when the procedure calls for them.

InputHow to prepare it
Collected adhesive massMeasure collected adhesive mass in grams on a suitable scale and preserve the sample boundary.
Collection timeObserve several cycles for collection time, then use a representative pace rather than the single fastest cycle.
Active nozzlesCount active nozzles in the exact operating unit shown on the dispatch or inventory record; do not mix eaches, packs, cases, or layers.
Target flow per nozzleUse the controlled target flow per nozzle in grams per minute per active nozzle for this exact setup.
Flow toleranceBase flow tolerance on measured comparable work, state what the percentage covers, and review it when the process changes.

How the calculation works

Observed flow per nozzle = collected grams ÷ seconds × 60 ÷ active nozzles
  1. Validate the Adhesive Output Calibration Checker manifest: confirm that collected adhesive mass, collection time, active nozzles describe the same Adhesive Output Calibration Checker pack, batch, or planning period.
  2. Calculate observed nozzle flow: apply Observed flow per nozzle = collected grams ÷ seconds × 60 ÷ active nozzles without rounding intermediate values for Adhesive Output Calibration Checker.
  3. Review the observed nozzle flow breakdown: use the primary result for the stated decision and the secondary values to identify the input or constraint driving this Adhesive Output Calibration Checker result.

Worked example

Collecting 48 g in 20 seconds from two nozzles equals 72 g/min per nozzle, 4% below a 75 g/min target and within a user-entered 5% tolerance.

Next action: After: retain the sample record, repeat as required, and escalate any variance through the equipment procedure.

How to interpret the result

A within-tolerance result describes only the entered sample. An out-of-tolerance result calls for safe inspection and repeat measurement under the responsible process; it does not tell an operator which setting to change.

Common mistakes

  • Collecting adhesive without the approved guarding and burn controls.
  • Dividing by nozzles that were not active for the full sample.
  • Comparing with a target from another adhesive or setup.
  • Changing temperature or pressure solely from the calculator flag.

Assumptions and limitations

The checker does not evaluate temperature, pressure, viscosity, pattern placement, nozzle balance over time, bond quality, calibration traceability, or equipment safety.

Adhesive Output Calibration Checker estimate only: verify the Adhesive Output Calibration Checker observed nozzle flow with the physical pack or operating record and the current requirements governing this decision.
