What this calculator solves

Use Shipping Damage Rate after a defined review period to convert a shipment count and a consistently classified damage count into an observed operational rate. It differs from Packaging Failure Cost, which values direct consequences, and from Packaging Trial Comparison, which compares two controlled variants on multiple criteria.

How to choose the inputs

Choose one population boundary—for example delivered parcels for one SKU family and one month—and apply the same rule to the numerator and denominator. Count each shipment once even if it contains several damaged units, unless the written metric explicitly uses units instead. Enter zero damage only when the complete reviewed sample has no recorded incident.

How the calculation works

Observed damage rate = damaged shipments ÷ shipments reviewed × 100
  1. Validate the Shipping Damage Rate Calculator manifest: confirm that shipments reviewed, shipments with observed damage describe the same Shipping Damage Rate Calculator pack, batch, or planning period.
  2. Calculate observed damage rate: apply Observed damage rate = damaged shipments ÷ shipments reviewed × 100 without rounding intermediate values for Shipping Damage Rate Calculator.
  3. Review the observed damage rate breakdown: use the primary result for the stated decision and the secondary values to identify the input or constraint driving this Shipping Damage Rate Calculator result.

Worked example

In a documented 30-day review, 14 of 1,250 delivered shipments had product or package damage under the team’s written definition. The observed rate is 1.12%, with 1,236 shipments not recorded as damaged and one observed incident per 89.3 reviewed shipments.

How to interpret the result

Trend the rate only across periods with the same damage definition, delivery-status boundary, and data completeness. A lower rate is favorable evidence, but a small sample or reporting delay can move it sharply. Investigate by SKU, pack method, fulfillment location, or handling path before attributing a change to packaging alone.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing damaged units with total shipments.
  • Counting replacements while omitting the original damaged shipment population.
  • Changing the damage definition between periods.
  • Treating an observed rate as a guarantee of future shipment performance.

Assumptions and limitations

The calculation is descriptive and does not adjust for claim lag, unreported damage, returns unrelated to packaging, carrier mix, product mix, exposure severity, or statistical uncertainty. It does not diagnose cause or establish an acceptable industry rate.

Shipping Damage Rate Calculator estimate only: verify the Shipping Damage Rate Calculator observed damage rate with the physical pack or operating record and the current requirements governing this decision.
