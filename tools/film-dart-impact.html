What this tool solves

A falling-dart staircase produces a sequence of masses and specimen outcomes, not a single breaking weight. This worksheet checks the recorded step directions and summarizes one completed series with a Dixon–Mood point estimate and a tally at each observed mass.

How to choose the inputs

Enter one test series in chronological order after applying the lab’s own series selection. Use total dart mass, including the carrier and added weights, and enter the same increment used throughout. Use a separate run for a different fixture, method, material, thickness, or drop height. The worksheet requires at least ten of each outcome.

How the calculation works

Check that each recorded mass is one step higher after a pass or one step lower after a failure. If failures are no more frequent than passes, estimate the center as the mean failure mass minus half a step; otherwise use the mean pass mass plus half a step. This is an exploratory up-and-down estimator, not a standard-specific reduction rule.

Worked example

The synthetic sequence alternates 100 g passes and 110 g failures ten times at a 10 g increment. Failures and passes are tied, so the worksheet uses 110 − 5 = 105 g. This is a point estimate, not an observed failure at 105 g.

How to interpret the output

Use the mass-level counts to inspect how much evidence supports the estimate. A narrow observed mass range does not give a confidence interval. A film dart mass is not a complete package drop height, puncture guarantee, or transport rating.

Common mistakes

  • Do not paste the table sorted by mass; the sequence is required.
  • Use total dart mass rather than the mass of the removable weights alone.
  • Do not pool different methods or use fixed-mass grouped trials in this staircase worksheet.

Assumptions and limitations

This tool does not select valid rows, assess the shape of the failure distribution, estimate confidence bounds, or implement all ASTM D1709 or ISO 7765 reporting and test rules. Use the instrument and approved analysis for a specification result.
