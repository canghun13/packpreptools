What this tool solves

A film can take one amount of force to start sliding and a different amount to keep moving. This worksheet reduces paired pull-force readings to static and kinetic coefficients of friction (COF), so a packer or film technician can review the spread before comparing material lots.

How to choose the inputs

Use the total moving mass that supplies the normal load on a horizontal test surface. Enter the starting peak and the steady-sliding force selected by your lab for each specimen. Use one force unit and one sled mass per run. Keep the contacting surfaces, their orientation and the conditioning together in the lab record.

How the calculation works

Convert pull forces to newtons and divide each by the normal force: sled mass in kilograms × 9.80665 m/s². The two ratios are unitless. Calculate the mean and range for each set separately, without combining the static and kinetic columns.

Worked example

For a 200 g sled the normal force is 1.96133 N. A 0.60 N starting peak and 0.40 N sliding force give 0.3059 static and 0.2039 kinetic COF. The prefilled replicates have the same means and show the spread around them.

How to interpret the output

Use the ranges to decide whether to review individual specimens rather than relying on an average. The worksheet counts kinetic readings above static without dropping them. Film-to-metal and film-to-film results, or opposite faces of a film, are not interchangeable.

Common mistakes

  • Enter gram-force readings as gf, not as the sled mass or as newtons.
  • Do not substitute an arbitrary force point for the steady sliding interval.
  • Do not mix surface pairs, directions, or test speeds in one summary.

Assumptions and limitations

This reduces existing horizontal sled readings. It does not choose a friction target, predict jams, set equipment, or establish that a test met ASTM D1894 or another procedure.
